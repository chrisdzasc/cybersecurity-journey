## Module 1 – Introduction to Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity is the continuous effort to protect people, organizations and governments from digital attacks by keeping networked systems and data safe from unauthorized access or damage. The data at risk can be personal (any information that identifies you, online or offline) or organizational (transactional data, intellectual property and financial data). Protecting it relies on three principles: confidentiality, integrity and availability (the CIA triad). Attackers are individuals or groups who exploit vulnerabilities for personal or financial gain, and threats can be internal (employees, contractors or trusted partners) or external (cybercriminals, hacktivists or state-sponsored groups).

## Module 2 – Attacks, Concepts and Techniques

Attackers look for security vulnerabilities, software or hardware weaknesses that can be exploited, and use exploits or malware to steal data, bypass access controls or compromise systems. It also reviews the most common attack methods, from social engineering and phishing to denial-of-service attacks, and how they can be combined into more complex threats.

## Module 3 – Protecting Your Data and Privacy

Online security means taking practical steps to keep personal information out of the wrong hands: enabling the firewall, installing antivirus and antispyware, keeping the operating system and browser up to date, and protecting devices with passwords. Accounts should use unique, strong passwords reinforced with two-factor authentication, and regular backups help prevent the loss of irreplaceable data.

## Module 4 – Protecting the Organization

Organizations defend their networks with layered security appliances, either dedicated devices or software running on them: routers, firewalls (from basic packet filtering to next-generation firewalls), intrusion detection and prevention systems, VPNs and antimalware. Because attacks move fast, real-time detection and behavior-based security help spot anomalies as they happen, often using tools like NetFlow to establish what normal traffic looks like. Beyond technology, organizations need a plan to prepare for, respond to and recover from a breach: assessing and managing risk, testing defenses through penetration testing, and relying on a CSIRT and security playbooks to respond consistently.

## Module 5 – Will Your Future Be in Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity professionals develop powerful skills that can be used ethically or illegally, so the guiding principle is simple: just because you can, doesn't mean you should. Breaking cybersecurity laws can get the organization penalized and cost you your job, or even lead to prosecution, fines and prison, so when in doubt about whether an action is legal, assume it isn't and don't do it.

## Key Concepts

<details>
<summary><b>Module 1 – Introduction to Cybersecurity</b></summary>
  
- **Cybersecurity:** the ongoing effort to protect individuals, organizations and governments from digital attacks by protecting networked systems and data from unauthorized use or harm.
- **Personal data:** any information that can be used to identify you; it can exist both offline and online.
- **Offline identity:** the real-life persona you present on a daily basis.
- **Online identity:** who you are and how you present yourself to others online.
- **Confidentiality:** a set of rules that prevents sensitive information from being disclosed to unauthorized people, resources and processes.
- **Integrity:** ensures that system information and processes are protected from intentional or accidental modification.
- **Availability:** ensures that authorized users can access systems and data when and where they need them, while those who don't meet the established conditions cannot.
- **Security breach:** an incident that results in unauthorized access to data, applications, services or devices, exposing private information that attackers can use for financial gain or other advantages.
- **Attackers:** individuals or groups who attempt to exploit vulnerabilities for personal or financial gain.
- **Script kiddies (amateurs):** inexperienced hackers who use existing tools or instructions found on the internet to launch attacks.
- **White hat hackers:** break into networks or computer systems to identify weaknesses so their security can be improved.
- **Gray hat hackers:** may set out to find vulnerabilities in a system, but only report them to the owners if it suits their agenda, or may even publish them online so other hackers can exploit them.
- **Black hat hackers:** take advantage of any vulnerability for illegal personal, financial or political gain.
- **Hacktivists:** attackers who make political statements to raise awareness of issues that are important to them.
- **Cyberwarfare:** the use of technology to penetrate and attack another nation's computer systems and networks to cause damage or disrupt services.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Module 2 – Attacks, Concepts and Techniques</b></summary>

- **Hardware vulnerabilities:** weaknesses that result from hardware design flaws.
- **Software vulnerabilities:** weaknesses introduced by errors in the operating system or application code.
- **Malware:** any code that can be used to steal data, bypass access controls, or harm or compromise a system.
- **Spyware:** malware designed to track and spy on the user.
- **Adware:** software, often bundled with other programs, designed to automatically deliver advertisements, usually in the web browser.
- **Backdoor:** a way to gain unauthorized access by bypassing the normal authentication procedures of a system.
- **Ransomware:** malware that holds a computer system or its data captive until a payment is made.
- **Scareware:** malware that uses scare tactics to trick the user into taking a specific action.
- **Rootkit:** malware that modifies the operating system to create a backdoor attackers can use to access the computer remotely.
- **Virus:** a program that, when executed, replicates by attaching its own code to other executable files, such as documents.
- **Trojan horse:** malware that carries out malicious operations by disguising its true intent.
- **Worm:** malware that replicates itself to spread from one computer to another.
- **Cryptojacking:** malware that hides on a device and uses its resources to mine cryptocurrency without the owner's knowledge or consent.
- **Social engineering:** the manipulation of people into performing actions or revealing confidential information.
- **Phishing:** a social engineering attack that uses fraudulent messages, usually emails, to trick people into revealing information or installing malware.
- **Denial of Service (DoS):** an attack that interrupts network services to users, devices or applications.
- **Distributed DoS (DDoS):** similar to a DoS, but launched from multiple coordinated sources.
- **Botnet:** a group of infected devices (bots) connected through the internet and controlled by a malicious individual or group.
- **On-path attack:** intercepts or modifies communications between two devices to collect information or impersonate one of them.
- **SEO poisoning:** abusing search engine optimization on popular search terms to push malicious sites higher in search results.
- **Password attacks:** techniques used to discover or bypass a user's password in order to access their accounts and information.
- **Password spraying:** trying a few commonly used passwords across many accounts.
- **Dictionary attack:** trying every word in a dictionary or a list of common passwords.
- **Brute-force attack:** trying every possible combination of letters, numbers and symbols until the password is found.
- **Rainbow table attack:** comparing a password's hash against precomputed hashes stored in a rainbow table.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Module 3 – Protecting your Data and Privacy</b></summary>

- **Encryption:** converting information into a form unauthorized parties cannot read; only someone with the secret key or password can decrypt it and access the original data.
- **Terms of service:** a legally binding contract that governs the relationship between you, the service provider and others who use the service.
- **Data use policy:** outlines how the service provider collects, uses and shares your data.
- **Privacy settings:** let you control who can see information about you and access your profile or account data.
- **Security policy:** outlines what the company does to secure the data it collects from you.
- **Two-factor authentication (2FA):** in addition to a username and password or PIN, requires a second token to verify your identity.
- **OAuth (Open Authorization):** an open protocol that lets you use your existing credentials to access third-party applications without exposing your password.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Module 4 – Protecting the organization</b></summary>


- **Port number:** an identifier assigned to each application running on a device so network traffic reaches the right service.
- **Firewall:** controls or filters which communications are allowed into and out of a device or network.
- **Network layer firewall:** filters communications based on source and destination IP addresses.
- **Transport layer firewall:** filters communications based on source and destination ports and connection states.
- **Application layer firewall:** filters communications based on an application, program or service.
- **Context-aware application firewall:** filters communications based on the user, device, role, application type and threat profile.
- **Proxy server:** filters web content requests such as URLs, domain names and media types.
- **Reverse proxy server:** placed in front of web servers to protect, hide, offload and distribute access to them.
- **NAT firewall:** hides or masquerades the private addresses of network hosts.
- **Host-based firewall:** filters ports and system service calls on a single computer.
- **Port scanning:** probing a computer, server or other host for open ports.
- **Intrusion Detection System (IDS):** compares traffic against a database of rules or attack signatures to detect, log and report malicious activity.
- **Intrusion Prevention System (IPS):** can block or deny traffic when it matches a rule or signature.
- **Real-time detection:** the ability to detect attacks as they happen and stop them immediately or within minutes.
- **Behavior-based security:** detects threats by capturing and analyzing communication flows and flagging deviations from normal patterns as anomalies.
- **Honeypot:** a decoy system set up to look like a high-value asset, such as a server, to attract and study attackers.
- **NetFlow:** gathers information about data flowing through a network: which users and devices are on it, and when and how they access it.
- **Penetration testing:** assessing a system, network or organization for security vulnerabilities by simulating an attack.
  - **Planning:** gathering as much information as possible about the target, its potential vulnerabilities and the exploits to use.
  - **Scanning:** active reconnaissance to probe the target and identify weaknesses that could give an attacker access.
  - **Gaining access:** attempting to access the target system and sniff network traffic.
  - **Maintaining access:** keeping access to find out which data and systems are vulnerable.
  - **Analysis and reporting:** documenting the findings and providing recommendations.
- **Risk management:** the formal, continuous process of identifying and assessing risk to reduce the impact of threats and vulnerabilities.
  - **Frame the risk:** identify the threats that increase risk.
  - **Assess the risk:** determine the severity of each threat.
  - **Respond to the risk:** create an action plan to reduce risk exposure by avoiding, mitigating, transferring or accepting it.
  - **Monitor the risk:** continuously review the risks that were addressed.
- **CSIRT (Computer Security Incident Response Team):** a team that receives, reviews and responds to security incident reports.
- **Security playbook:** a collection of repeatable queries or reports that outline a standardized process for incident detection and response.
- **SIEM (Security Information and Event Management):** collects and analyzes security alerts, logs and other real-time and historical data from network security devices to detect attacks early.
- **Data Loss Prevention (DLP):** designed to stop sensitive data from being stolen from or leaking out of a network.

</details>
